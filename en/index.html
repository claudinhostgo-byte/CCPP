Our foundation

What's committed, and the new context

We start from what the school itself states and signs —we're not asking for the impossible: we're asking for what was already committed.

The service agreement (clause 6) commits to: a demanding academic level with an emphasis on English, qualified staff, infrastructure (classrooms, library, lab, and sports field), extracurricular activities, and information and participation for families through the Parents' Association.
The transition to Nord Anglia: the school's entry into an international education group opens up opportunities, but also raises legitimate questions about the continuity of the project, tuition, and management. We propose that the Parents' Association act as a formal, informed, and collaborative counterpart during this transition: channeling families' concerns in an orderly way, and ensuring that commitments already made by the school are upheld.
Urgent

Protecting now, without waiting for budget

Safety and wellbeing measures that can start immediately, at low cost and with high impact for children.

🛡️

Safe recess and supervision

  • Visible presence of responsible adults during recess; supervision map of yards, hallways, restrooms, and critical areas.
  • Incident logging and follow-up.
  • Regular meetings between Student Affairs, School Climate, and the Association's Board.
  • Ensure a responsible school staff member is present, with Association parents as complementary support.
Reduce risks, conflicts, and unsupervised spaces.
🤝

School climate, bullying and the digital world

  • Clear, simple communication of school climate, bullying, and cyberbullying protocols.
  • Talks for students and parents on social media, phones, and digital respect.
  • A clear Association support channel to report incidents, with response timelines and follow-up.
  • Ongoing link with the Red Preventiva (drug and alcohol prevention network), active in over 80 schools.
That no student faces mistreatment or exclusion without support.
🚨

Major emergency plan

  • Review, update, and communication of the Comprehensive Safety Plan (fire, earthquake, gas leak, external threat, medical emergency).
  • A family plan: what to do, where to get information, and how reunification with students will work.
  • At least one annual drill with family participation and a follow-up review.
That safety protocols are known across the whole community, not just internally.
🚗

Safe pickup and traffic campaign

  • Waiting and pickup zones by grade level; orderly traffic, drop-off, and workshop flow, with a focus on March.
  • Ongoing road-safety campaign at entrances with the Student Council (CCAA); assess signage and traffic lights.
  • Push the school for an access-control and pickup system, with strict protection of students' personal data (Law 21.719).
A predictable, safe, and orderly arrival and departure.
♿

Real inclusion

  • Standing committee for inclusion and support for students with diverse needs (ASD, ADHD, neurodivergence, disability).
  • A voluntary, confidential channel to advise families who wish to use it; the Association will not request student data from the school.
  • Awareness-building for students, families, and the community; meaningful participation in sports, workshops, and events.
  • A bridge for the school to enable, on campus, activities linked to students' therapies and support needs.
That every child can take part, grow, and feel they belong.
Relevant

This year, with coordination and some investment

Initiatives with a direct impact on students' and families' daily life, requiring study, agreements, and management.

🍽️

Food and lunch

  • Organize schedules and support younger students.
  • Study of interest and demand by grade level.
  • Evaluate models: concession, outside provider, mixed, or phased approach.
  • Safe options for heating food brought from home while there's no formal service.
A safe, sustainable service, with no improvisation or hidden costs.
🥗

Healthy food, kiosk & vending

  • Pilot kiosk run by 2° Medio (senior year) students —subject to school authorization and a health review— as advance fundraising for their class trip, with transparent accounting.
  • Healthy vending with an outside provider (water, yogurt, nuts, bars), run by 1° Medio students to fund their own trip.
  • Initial funding to be defined: a recoverable Association contribution, sponsorship, or a provider loan.
  • Options for dietary restrictions and accessible prices.
Make the healthy choice easy, permanent, and low operational risk.
🚻

Dignified restrooms

  • Assessment of cleaning frequency and supply restocking.
  • A simple channel to report restrooms in poor condition during the day.
  • Compliance indicators reviewed with the administration.
Clean, dignified, and safe restrooms throughout the school day.
💉

Health and prevention

  • Continue the annual flu vaccination drive, open to the community.
  • Support for the Nursing office and World Health Day activities.
  • Coordination with School Climate and the school's Red Preventiva.
Care for community health with concrete, regular actions.
👨‍👩‍👧

Parenting School

  • Parenting, boundaries, adolescence, self-esteem, and mental health.
  • Screens, social media, alcohol/drugs, and emotional intelligence.
  • In-person and online formats, with short video segments and downloadable material (including Red Preventiva resources).
Practical tools to raise and support our children today.
📊

Transparency and funding

  • Regular surveys; continuity of the monthly newsletter and the Association's Instagram; a biannual public report.
  • Working groups with owners and deadlines, keeping the existing monthly meetings with school leadership (delegate form → response and minutes), adding response timelines.
  • Strengthen the Accounts Review Committee and the network of class delegates and sub-delegates; network with the Providencia campus Association and other parent associations in the area.
  • Propose that the school offer, when paying annual tuition, a voluntary option to contribute the Association's fee (current fee: CLP $30,000/year) — never mandatory.
  • Promote a Chicureo Parents' Associations Roundtable to collaborate with other communities in the area.
Represent families, report results, and secure sustainable funding.
🎓

Support for classes and study trips

  • Best-practices guide for fundraising and training for class representatives.
  • Transparent-accounting models and a directory of vendors/sponsors.
  • Savings plans starting in the lower grades, with ongoing fundraising activities for the senior (2° Medio) trip, keeping the families' contribution as low as possible.
Help classes get organized and support their projects.
🏃

Sports and active living

  • Ongoing support for all sports programs (soccer, rugby, swimming, athletics) and their fundraising.
  • Push for more sports teaching staff.
  • Internal championships, family events, and inclusive sports.
Healthier, more disciplined, and engaged students.
Important

Medium and long term: investment and community

Strategic infrastructure, culture, and community projects that build the school's and families' future.

💻

Technology & modernization

  • Assessment of the computer lab, connectivity, and equipment.
  • Gradual renewal of equipment and digital tools.
  • Robotics, coding, and educational use of technology.
Prepare students for a real technological world.
🤖

AI & digital citizenship

  • Responsible AI use: telling apart help, research, and copying.
  • Workshops for parents on AI, parental controls, and privacy.
  • Digital critical thinking and online safety.
Technology as a learning tool, not a risk.
📚

Library & culture

  • Improvement of the library and reading spaces.
  • Updated children's, young-adult, and English-language books.
  • Reading clubs, book fairs, and writing workshops.
Strengthen reading, culture, English, and critical thinking.
🌱

Sustainability and science

  • Continuity for the current Sustainability and Science Committee: science fairs, recycling, and environmental education.
  • Activities that connect science and the environment to school life, in coordination with the school.
Keep the environmental and scientific work already underway alive.
🤲

Solidarity and support

  • Continue the Solidarity Committee: monthly donations to shelters (e.g., Hogar San Ricardo) and fundraising for the Teletón.
  • Collection drives; a solidarity network for complex family situations, with privacy and respect.
  • Community service and visits to supported shelters and daycares.
Mobilize our children through concrete acts of solidarity.
💼

Benefits, partnerships & entrepreneurship network

  • Maintain and expand family benefits and partnerships (sports, health, local businesses and services) through a member card.
  • Volunteer directory of parents' professions and businesses; a mentorship and collaboration network.
  • A Lost & Found committee and periodic sales of used uniforms.
A community that knows, collaborates, and supports each other, with real benefits.
🎉

Social & family life

  • Continuity for Café Concert, Bingo Trewheliano, Chilean Heritage Day/Fonda, and Parents Games.
  • Family activities: fair, family fun run, outdoor movie nights, and Family Day.
Strengthen bonds, belonging, and closeness.
New strategic pillar

Future, innovation, and global opportunities

Just as we'll push for safety, inclusion, and wellbeing, we also want to make sure every Trewhela's student can benefit from the academic, technological, athletic, and global opportunities that Nord Anglia makes available to our school community.

Tracking the Nord Anglia rollout MIT and STEAM culture Global Campus UNICEF and social leadership IMG sports opportunities Global citizenship AI and future skills Communication and equitable access
🌉

Future & Innovation Committee

  • Explain new Nord Anglia initiatives to the community and spread the word on opportunities.
  • Answer questions and surface families' concerns.
  • Monitor how programs are rolled out.
Be the bridge between Trewhela's, Nord Anglia, and families.
🔎

Nord Anglia Observatory

  • A biannual report on programs implemented and student participation.
  • Benefits gained and future opportunities.
  • Keeps this information from staying only with school leadership.
Build transparency around the real progress of joining the network.
🚀

Trewhela's STEAM Program

  • An annual STEAM fair and innovation week, inspired by MIT and STEAM Festivals.
  • Inter-class challenges and participation from parent professionals.
  • Mentorships in engineering, medicine, technology, and science.
Connects with families' entrepreneurship and professional network.
🌍

Global Opportunities Roadmap

  • Ask the school to report each year on MIT, Global Campus, UNICEF, STEAM Festivals, IMG Academy, Summer Programs, and international expeditions.
  • With dates, spots available, requirements, and costs.
So no family finds out late or by chance about these opportunities.

Roadmap

How we'll make it happen

A simple methodology for each pillar: diagnosis, validation with the school, cost assessment, phased rollout, and public follow-up.

1
First 30 days

Diagnosis

Surveys, needs assessment, and review of priorities by grade level.

→ A real agenda of priority topics.
2
45 to 60 days

Working groups

Meetings with the school on safety, food, school climate, inclusion, technology, and community.

→ Owners and formal agreements.
3
60 to 90 days

Financial assessment

Costs, providers, infrastructure, and sustainability for initiatives with financial impact.

→ Viable, phased proposals.
4
Throughout the year

Implementation

Controlled pilots and gradual improvements.

→ Measurable results, no improvisation.
5
Monthly / biannual

Reporting

Bulletins, public reports, and follow-up on commitments.

→ Trust and transparency.
Note: the Urgent / Relevant / Important classification is a working proposal based on the priority order in the source document (students first, then families, then community). It may be adjusted based on validation with the school and parent surveys.
